# Brome Squirrel Solution 200

## What it is

A midsize hanging tube feeder from Brome’s Squirrel Solution line (SKU **2004-V03** on the Canada product table). Six seed ports sit behind a metal shroud/cage. Openings in the shroud line up with the ports for birds; when a squirrel climbs on, its weight forces the shroud down and closes access. Brome’s Canada page marks this model **NON-ADJUSTABLE**.

This page is not a lab test. We did not hang one. Specs below are from Brome’s US and Canada product pages and one independent 1-week test of eight feeders.

## Who it's for

People who want a hang tube with a visible metal cage and the same weight-closes-ports idea as the Squirrel Buster line, and who do not need an adjustable spring. Bob Vila’s tester saw nuthatches, finches, and chickadees use it. Brome’s bird list on the US and Canada pages includes Northern Cardinal, goldfinch, chickadee, woodpeckers, and blue jay among others. Placement copy matches the rest of the family: window, tree, deck, fence, garden, house, rain trough, or sliding doors, with **18 inches** of clearance around it.

## Standouts

- Weight-activated shroud. Brome: squirrel weight forces the shroud down and closes the ports. Bob Vila’s table calls the type “Cage and tension system.” The exclusion mechanism is still weight on the feeder, not cage openings sized to keep squirrels out like a pure caged suet model.
- Six ports and midsize capacity. Brome CA table: **2 qts / 3.4 lbs**, dimensions **140 mm × 140 mm × 610 mm**, weight **1.23 kg**. Brome US store leads with **3.4 lbs** and six ports. Bob Vila’s comparison table lists **2 quarts**. We are not converting those units past what the pages print.
- Chew-proof claim. Brome CA: parts exposed to squirrels use chew-proof materials. Bob Vila names RoxResin and calls this pick Best Chew-Proof after the eight-feeder, one-week test. That is a short-test award, not a multi-season chew study.
- Seed Tube Ventilation on the Canada page (humidity and hot air out the top). Bob Vila also notes a ventilation system that keeps seed dry.
- Detachable by hand, no tools, for cleaning (Canada page). Cornell FeederWatch wants seed feeders cleaned about every week or two.
- Free seed funnel is named on retailer and Amazon-adjacent packaging we saw in research; Brome’s US and Canada product pages we fetched do not clearly show the funnel on the main copy, so funnel inclusion from the manufacturer pages alone is **unknown**.
- Lifetime Care is Brome’s support line, Monday–Friday 9 a.m.–5 p.m. EST, except holidays. That is a phone promise, not a measured lifespan.
- Bob Vila’s tester: tension-spring system foiled squirrels in the test week; size and design regularly lured nuthatches, finches, and chickadees. Works best with black-oil sunflower in their write-up.

## Drawbacks

- Brome’s own disclaimer: this feeder will not deter grackles, starlings, juvenile red/Douglas/flying squirrels, chipmunks, possums, bears, deer, or raccoons. They tell you to bring it in at night.
- **NON-ADJUSTABLE** on the Canada page. If you need a spring dial for larger songbirds vs squirrels, look at adjustable siblings already listed (Plus), not this SKU.
- Bob Vila noticed larger songbirds like cardinals and titmice seemed reluctant on the smaller perches, even though Brome’s bird list includes Northern Cardinal. We leave that conflict on the page. Cardinal-friendly perch: **unknown** from these sources.
- If a squirrel can reach the ports without putting its weight on the feeder, the shroud does not close. Brome wants at least 18 inches of clearance. Cornell FeederWatch still says squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders and that baffles plus about 10 feet from a jump-off are usually the better defense.
- Bob Vila tested eight feeders for one week. That is short. “Best chew-proof” is a useful data point, not a durability study.
- Hang only as the primary mount on these sources. Do not treat it as a pole system.
- Brome’s US store listed **$90.95** on 2026-09-05. Brome Canada listed **$106.45 CAD** the same day. Those are two store prices that day, not a survey. We are not ranking value.

## Who should skip

- You need adjustable spring tension. Get the Plus listing, not this one.
- You cannot hang it with roughly 18 inches of clear air around it.
- Your problem is grackles or starlings. Brome says this model does not stop them.
- You need a pure caged suet feeder, a motorized spinner, or a pole-and-baffle dome. Those are different mechanisms (already listed elsewhere on this site).
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears hibernate.
- You mainly want large-perch cardinal feeding and Bob Vila’s reluctance note worries you. Treat cardinal use as unproven here.

## Verdict

If you want a midsize weight-activated hang tube with a metal cage shroud and Bob Vila’s Best Chew-Proof label from a one-week, eight-feeder test, this is that SKU. The mechanism is real. Brome publishes the animals it will not stop and marks the spring non-adjustable on the Canada page. Cornell still treats “squirrel-proof” as a claim squirrels often beat. Buy it as resistant hardware plus placement, not as a spell.
