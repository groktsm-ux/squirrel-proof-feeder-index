# Duncraft Pavilion Haven Bird Feeder, Pole Mount

## What it is

A small **roofed platform tray inside wire fencing, sold with its own pole**. Duncraft sells it as **Duncraft Pavilion Haven Bird Feeder, Pole Mount**, SKU **4579** (GTIN **742933045795** in the page schema). Duncraft's description: "Our anchored haven wire fencing with 1-1/2 inch fly-through openings keeps out gray squirrels and larger birds." It has a "clearview plastic roof," "metal mesh flooring" for drainage that you remove for cleaning, and it "Includes 72 inch tall black powder coated steel sectional pole." Body is "Durable green recycled plastic," **12 x 12 x 10-3/4 inches**, and it "Holds 1-1/2 lbs. of seed." Duncraft says it is made in the USA and sold only by Duncraft.

Duncraft specifications table: Mount Type **Post**, Material **Recycled Plastic, Plastic**, Feeder Type **Platform Feeder**, Feed Type **Black Oil Sunflower Seed, Whole Sunflower Hearts, Specialty Blends & Mixes, No-Waste No-Mess Seeds**, Bird Species **Cardinals, Chickadees, Finches, Nuthatches, Wrens**.

Why it is a different caged form from what is already listed: our other eleven caged listings hang (tubes, suet cages), stick to a window (Duncraft Sheltered Haven) or sit on the ground (Kingsyard caged ground tray). This is the first one that comes on a pole. It is caged, not pole+baffle: the copy names wire fencing as the squirrel barrier and does not mention a baffle. Duncraft sells a ground version (Ground Platform Haven Feeder) and other "Haven" cages; we did not list or compare them tonight.

This page is not a lab test. We did not set the feeder out. Facts come from the Duncraft product page and Cornell FeederWatch. We found no named hands-on test of this model. Duncraft's page shows a store star rating from 3 reviews; store ratings are not evidence and we do not score them.

## Who it's for

People who want to offer seed on a covered tray to small birds, keep gray squirrels and larger birds out with a cage rather than a moving part, and want the pole included rather than bought separately.

## Standouts

- The mechanism matches the bucket: fencing with **1-1/2 inch** fly-through openings that Duncraft says keeps out gray squirrels and larger birds. Duncraft calls the fencing "anchored," meaning attached; the page does not say how.
- Comes with a **72 inch** sectional steel pole (Duncraft description).
- Clear roof over the seed and a metal mesh floor for drainage. Duncraft says the mesh floor comes out for cleaning.
- Cornell FeederWatch names "feeders surrounded by a wire mesh large enough for only small birds to get through" as one way to deter larger birds, which is what this fencing does.
- No batteries or springs.
- Guarantee: Duncraft's site FAQ on the page describes a **365-Day Guarantee** ("We'll help you exchange it or find the right fit for your birds"). We did not fetch a separate guarantee document, so the full terms are **unknown** here.
- Price: **$159.95** on Duncraft on **2026-10-27**. That is one store on one fetch day, not a price survey.

## Drawbacks

- No baffle. Nothing in Duncraft's copy mentions a pole baffle. Cornell FeederWatch says baffles are usually the best way to keep squirrels away and, on pole-mounted feeders, can go beneath the feeder to stop climbing. Whether squirrels that climb the pole can bother the cage, roof or seed from outside is **unknown**; Duncraft does not say.
- Cardinal conflict, left unresolved. Duncraft's Bird Species field lists **Cardinals**. The openings are **1-1/2 inch**. A different maker, Kingsyard, told a customer on its own caged feeder page that "adult cardinals are typically too large to fit through 1.5" openings" (see our Kingsyard caged ground listing). We don't know which is true for this cage, so we mark cardinal access **unknown**.
- Stock conflict, left unresolved. The visible page says the feeder "is backordered and will ship as soon as it is back in stock" and shows "Unavailable" by the price. The product schema on the same page says **InStock**. Shipping weight in the product data is **0** lb, which can't be right; the real weight is **unknown**.
- Small capacity: **1-1/2 lbs** of seed (Duncraft description).
- It is a tray. Cornell FeederWatch notes tray feeders collect bird droppings and suggests retiring tray feeders if you see diseases spread that way (salmonella, trichomoniasis). Cornell says clean seed feeders every week or two.
- Naming: Duncraft files it under squirrel-proof feeders and says the fencing "keeps out gray squirrels." The copy says nothing about red squirrels or chipmunks; that is **unknown**. Cornell: squirrels often find a way into "squirrel-proof" feeders. We say resistant.
- Pole height vs placement. Duncraft gives a 72 inch pole. Cornell says squirrels can jump to feeders less than ten feet from a tree or building, and suggests about 10 feet from cover. Duncraft's page gives no clearance advice for this feeder.

## Who should skip

- You mainly want cardinals or other larger birds at this feeder, until the cardinal conflict above is settled.
- Your squirrels already climb poles and you don't plan to add a baffle.
- You want a hanging, window or ground caged feeder. Look at the other caged listings.
- You want a motorized spinning perch. The **Yankee Flipper** is our only motorized listing.
- You want a big-capacity feeder; this one holds 1-1/2 lbs.
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears are hibernating.

## Verdict

Twelfth caged listing and the first caged feeder on a pole: a roofed recycled-plastic tray behind 1-1/2 inch wire fencing, with a 72 inch pole, holding 1-1/2 lbs of seed. Resistant, not magic. There's no baffle in the copy, Duncraft's page lists cardinals while the openings are 1-1/2 inch, the stock status contradicts itself, and we found no hands-on test of this model.
