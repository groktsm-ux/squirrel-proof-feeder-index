# Wild Birds Unlimited Advanced Pole System (APS) Hanging Kit with APS Squirrel Baffle

## What it is

A ground-mounted **modular feeder pole** plus a **tapered steel pole baffle**, both sold under the **Wild Birds Unlimited** (WBU) name. WBU calls the pole the **Advanced Pole System®** (APS), "exclusive" and "patented," and says it is "available only at Wild Birds Unlimited." WBU's warranty page names the maker of the APS as **Holscher Products Inc.**

The two parts we are pairing, as WBU names them:

- **APS Hanging Kit.** The WBU product text still calls it the "Basic Setup." It includes a **48″ base pole**, a **48″ extension pole**, a **double crook arm**, a **two-piece large stabilizer** and a **chickadee finial**. "Just twist the 4-foot base pole into the ground using the convenient corkscrew auger. Next slide the Stabilizer onto the base pole and push into the ground." WBU: "Tests show the stabilizer holds the pole straight in up to 35 MPH wind gusts." WBU does not say who ran those tests. Feeders and food are not included.
- **APS Squirrel Baffle.** "Made with powder coated steel for rust resistance, UV protection and added durability, these baffles feature a tapered design that is very effective in stopping squirrels before they reach your feeders. The baffle collar needed to pole mount this item is included. Pole system, feeders, and bird food sold separately." The shop page has Specifications, Warranty and Materials tabs, but they did not load for us, so the baffle's **diameter, length, weight and model number are unknown**.

**The baffle is not in the kit.** WBU sells it separately, and the Danvers store page says "Baffle sold separately." This listing covers the two bought together, the way WBU's own copy says to use them.

WBU also sells an **APS Mounting Kit** (36″ base pole, 36″ extension, stabilizer, dual-mount flange for a house or hopper) and an **APS Deck Kit** (deck clamp, 24″ extension, short extended deck arm, finial). We did not list those tonight.

How it differs from what we already list: our other eleven pole+baffle listings are Birds Choice, Squirrel Stopper, Erva, Duncraft and Arundale systems sold online, mostly with a ground socket or sleeve. This is the first one sold only through one store chain, and the first that screws into the ground with a corkscrew auger and then braces with a separate stabilizer.

This page is not a lab test. We did not set up the pole. Facts come from WBU's national pages, two WBU franchise store pages, WBU's warranty page, two first-person owner write-ups and Cornell FeederWatch.

## Who it's for

People with a lawn spot at least about 10 feet from trees, decks and roofs who want one pole they can add arms and accessories to over time, and who have a Wild Birds Unlimited store to buy from. Soft or loamy ground suits the auger best; see the rocky-ground warning below.

## Standouts

- **One owner, three years.** Gardening Products Review (Monica Hemingway, Darien, CT store purchase) wrote "the baffle completely eliminates scavenging squirrels" and that squirrels climb into the baffle and "back out." The disclaimer says the opinions "are based on 3 years of use." This is one person's yard, not a controlled test.
- **A second owner, several months.** In the Daily Hampshire Gazette, Kathy Van Mullekom (bought at a WBU store in Virginia Beach) wrote that she twisted "the 48-inch base pole 16 inches into the ground," and "several months later, the pole still stands straight, despite gusty winds and drenching rains." She does not report on the baffle's squirrel results.
- **Placement instructions from WBU itself.** The Pearland, TX store page: put the baffle so its top is **4½′–5′** from the ground, and set the pole **10′** from "horizontal jumping points like a tree, deck or house." WBU's national squirrel page says squirrels "can jump up to five feet vertically and ten feet between trees or structures."
- **Guarantee.** WBU's warranty page: Holscher Products Inc. guarantees the APS "for 10 years from date of purchase" against manufacturing defects. "Rusting/Powder coating is covered for 1 year." A receipt is required. Whether that page's APS coverage includes the baffle is not stated (**unknown**).
- **Modular.** WBU says the setup can start as a single arm and expand to four (Danvers store), with accessories sold separately.
- No batteries, springs or moving parts beyond the baffle.

## Drawbacks

- **Baffle costs extra and prices are per store.** WBU's online shop says "Find My Store for pricing & availability." The only price we saw was the **Thornhill, Ontario** store: APS Squirrel Baffle **$54.99**, shown as **Temporarily Unavailable**, on 2026-10-26. The page does not state the currency. We did not get a Hanging Kit price (**unknown**). One store on one fetch day is not a price survey.
- **Store-only.** WBU's own pages say to "contact your local Wild Birds Unlimited Store for product availability" and that "some products are… not carried at all stores." If you have no WBU nearby, this may not be practical.
- **Rocky ground.** Gardening Products Review: "buried rocks can damage the pole and prevent it from screwing fully into the ground." Set the base perfectly vertical before stepping in the stabilizer, or the whole system ends up crooked.
- **Wind and load.** The same review warns that overloading can make the pole sway, "fall over or bend/break one of the arms," and used four guy cords to tent pegs; it calls guy wires "a necessity" if raccoons throw themselves at the pole. A 2017 commenter put pavers on the stabilizer feet against frost heave. Owner comments are anecdotes, not tests.
- **Rust.** A 2013 commenter on that review said a part rusted and would not detach after about four years. That matches WBU's shorter **1-year** powder-coat coverage, but it is one comment.
- **Placement conflict, left unresolved.** WBU Danvers store: "Provided a pole is setup **6-8 feet** from things like trees, shrubs and fences, a baffle will keep squirrels off your feeders." WBU Pearland store: **10′**. WBU national page: squirrels jump **ten feet** between structures. Cornell: about **10 feet**. Gardening Products Review: "10 feet or more."
- **Naming.** WBU national copy says squirrels defeat "many so-called 'squirrel proof' feeders"; the Pearland page calls the baffle "squirrel-resistant"; a store comment reply on Gardening Products Review calls the system "really is squirrel-proof if you set it up correctly." Cornell: squirrels often find a way into "squirrel-proof" feeders. We say resistant.
- **Review disclosure conflict, left unresolved.** Gardening Products Review's own disclaimer says it "purchased the APS feeder system and all accessories at regular price," while the site's footer disclaimer on the same page thanks "the manufacturer/distributor for giving us a free sample." The page does not say which applies to this review.
- **Baffle specs unknown.** No diameter, length, or weight limit printed on the pages we could read.

## Who should skip

- You have no Wild Birds Unlimited store nearby and want something shipped from a web store today. The already-listed **Squirrel Stopper Deluxe**, **Erva** stations and **Birds Choice** kits sell online with the baffle included.
- Your yard is rocky or very hard, where an auger base may not seat.
- You want a squirrel-resistant feeder rather than a pole. That's the weight-activated, caged or motorized buckets; the Yankee Flipper is still the only honest motorized listing.
- You can't keep about **10 feet** of jump-off clearance (Cornell, WBU Pearland).
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears hibernate.

## Verdict

Twelfth pole+baffle listing and the first sold only through a store chain — a screw-in auger pole with a stabilizer and a tapered steel baffle, backed by a 10-year frame guarantee and one owner's three-year account, but the baffle is a separate purchase, prices are set per store, and WBU prints no baffle dimensions. It resists squirrels when placed right; it won't stop every one.
