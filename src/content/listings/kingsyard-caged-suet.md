# Kingsyard Metal Caged Squirrel Proof Suet Feeder

## What it is

A hanging all-metal caged suet feeder from Kingsyard, sold on the brand store as Metal Caged Squirrel Proof Suet Feeder, SKU **KYBF123CP**. The brand page (browser pass after an empty first HTML fetch) lists capacity **2 suets**, dimensions **9.8"L × 9.8"W × 7.8"H**, material metal mesh, rust-resistant / weatherproof claims, hang mount, and cage openings **1.5" × 1.5"**. Colors on that page’s image labels: black, brown, green. Amazon ASIN B09PH4FNZS (green) matches the model family and adds a **9.8-inch** diameter top baffle and a bottom tray. The mechanism is the cage: small birds reach the cakes; grey squirrels are supposed to stay out. Brand home footer and product page both claim a lifetime warranty.

This page is not a lab test. We did not hang one. Specs below are from the Kingsyard product page (browser), brand footer, one Amazon product page that day, public excerpts of The Spruce’s tested roundup (direct page fetch returned 402 Payment Required), and Cornell FeederWatch.

## Who it's for

People who hang suet and want a metal cage between the cakes and the squirrels, especially for clinging birds. Kingsyard’s bird list on the product page: bluebirds, chickadees, finches, nuthatches, woodpeckers, wrens. Amazon’s target list names woodpecker, finch, chickadee, nuthatch, and sparrow. Those are manufacturer / retailer attract lists, not a census from our yard. The Spruce named this model Best Suet Feeder in its tested roundup and said woodpeckers and other small birds can feed through the grid while squirrels are discouraged. It is a hang feeder. Neither Kingsyard nor Amazon printed a jump-off distance on the copy we fetched. Cornell FeederWatch still uses about **10 feet** from trees or buildings as the usual jump-off buffer, and treats baffles as usually the better squirrel defense than “squirrel-proof” marketing.

## Standouts

- Mechanism is named and visible: an all-metal cage. Kingsyard: openings **1.5" × 1.5"**, holds two suet cakes “out of reach from gray squirrels.” Amazon: **1-1/2 inch** fly-through openings. The Spruce’s published copy says the cage grid measures **just over 1 inch** wide and tall. We are not picking a winner on opening size.
- The Spruce tested this feeder as part of its squirrel-proof roundup and named it Best Suet. Their published notes: holds two cakes; grid lets multiple birds feed; cover keeps cakes dry; drainage holes on the bottom tray are slightly small (they suggest drilling larger ones in wet climates); build can feel a bit rickety; birds may prefer an open suet cage, but squirrels stay off longer so the food lasts. That is one editorial test, not a durability study. We did not re-run it.
- Capacity is **2 suet cakes** / “2 suets” on Kingsyard and Amazon. No cake-count conflict. Brand dimensions: **9.8 × 9.8 × 7.8 in**.
- Removable roof for install, refill, and clean (Kingsyard and Amazon). Lifetime warranty claimed on the product page and brand footer. We did not invent warranty exclusions; the page did not spell them out beyond the claim.
- Second caged listing on this index after More Birds Squirrel-X Double Suet. Different brand, still hang suet, still cage-primary.

## Drawbacks

- Opening-size copy conflicts. Kingsyard and Amazon: **1.5" / 1-1/2"**. The Spruce: **just over 1 inch**. A third-party retailer paste we saw while searching printed **1.32" W × 1.75" L**. Leave all of that. Do not average them.
- Price conflicts on the same day. Kingsyard store: **$37.99**, JSON-LD InStock / Add to Cart on the browser pass. Amazon green ASIN B09PH4FNZS: **$28.99**, in stock that day. One store price is one store that day, not a survey.
- Kingsyard’s own FAQ caveat on the product page: **very small red squirrels might still occasionally gain access**. The Spruce also flagged smaller squirrels as a possible reach risk. Birds may use a caged suet feeder less than an open one because access is harder.
- Cardinal perch is not a real ring on this product. Cardinals are not on Kingsyard’s or Amazon’s bird lists for this SKU. Treat cardinal access as false for this cage.
- Cornell FeederWatch: squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders; clean seed or suet feeders about every week or two; if you live with bears, do not feed except when bears hibernate. Capsaicin seed treatments are unproven for bird safety on that page.
- We are not treating Kingsyard’s own marketing blog “30-day test” as an independent lab. Brand marketing is not Bob Vila / The Spruce / Birds & Blooms.

## Who should skip

- You need woodpeckers the size of a large pileated that will not fit the openings, or cardinals on a perch. This cage is sized for small clinging birds.
- You want a weight-activated tube or a motorized spinner as the main product. Those are different mechanisms.
- You want a pole-and-dome system as the main product. This is a hang suet cage.
- Your problem is tiny red squirrels or chipmunks. Kingsyard’s FAQ and The Spruce both leave that door open.
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears hibernate.

## Verdict

If you want a second caged hang suet feeder after Squirrel-X, this is the Kingsyard metal cage with a weather dome and two-cake capacity. The Spruce’s tested roundup liked it for keeping squirrels off suet. Opening size and same-day price disagree across pages. Kingsyard itself warns about very small red squirrels. Cornell still treats “squirrel-proof” as a claim squirrels often beat. Buy it as a cage plus placement, not as a spell.
