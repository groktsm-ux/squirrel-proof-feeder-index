# Kingsyard Metal Caged Squirrel Proof Suet Feeder

## What it is

A hanging all-metal caged suet feeder from Kingsyard, sold on the brand store as Metal Caged Squirrel Proof Suet Feeder (model family KYBF123CP). The Amazon listing for ASIN B09PH4FNZS (green) matches that model family and lists **2 suet cakes**, powder-coated metal, a removable roof, a **9.8-inch** diameter top baffle, and a bottom tray. The mechanism is a cage with fly-through openings: small birds are supposed to reach the cakes; adult squirrels are not. Hang mount. Brand home footer claims a lifetime warranty; the product page body returned almost empty on this fetch (JS-rendered), so detailed inches below come from the Amazon product bullets and The Spruce’s published test copy, not from a filled Kingsyard spec table.

This page is not a lab test. We did not hang one. Specs below are from the Kingsyard product URL and brand footer, one Amazon product page that day, public excerpts of The Spruce’s tested roundup (direct page fetch returned 402 Payment Required), and Cornell FeederWatch.

## Who it's for

People who hang suet and want a metal cage between the cakes and the squirrels, especially for clinging birds. Amazon’s target list names woodpecker, finch, chickadee, nuthatch, and sparrow. That is listing copy, not a census from our yard. The Spruce named this model Best Suet Feeder in its tested roundup and said woodpeckers and other small birds can feed through the grid while squirrels are discouraged. It is a hang feeder. Amazon does not print a jump-off distance on the bullets we fetched. Cornell FeederWatch still uses about **10 feet** from trees or buildings as the usual jump-off buffer, and treats baffles as usually the better squirrel defense than “squirrel-proof” marketing.

## Standouts

- Mechanism is named and visible: an all-metal cage plus a top weather baffle. Amazon says **1-1/2 inch** fly-through openings discourage adult squirrels while allowing downy woodpeckers and other small birds. The Spruce’s published copy says the cage grid measures **just over 1 inch** wide and tall. We are not picking a winner on opening size.
- The Spruce tested this feeder as part of its squirrel-proof roundup and named it Best Suet. Their published notes: holds two cakes; grid lets multiple birds feed; cover keeps cakes dry; drainage holes on the bottom tray are slightly small (they suggest drilling larger ones in wet climates); build can feel a bit rickety; birds may prefer an open suet cage, but squirrels stay off longer so the food lasts. That is one editorial test, not a durability study. We did not re-run it.
- Capacity is **2 suet cakes** on both the Kingsyard product title and the Amazon bullets. No cake-count conflict.
- Removable roof for refill and clean (Amazon). Brand home footer: lifetime warranty and 30-day free return language. We did not invent warranty exclusions; the product page body did not spell them out on this fetch.
- Second caged listing on this index after More Birds Squirrel-X Double Suet. Different brand, still hang suet, still cage-primary.

## Drawbacks

- Opening-size copy conflicts. Amazon: **1-1/2"**. The Spruce: **just over 1 inch**. A third-party retailer paste we saw while searching printed **1.32" W × 1.75" L**. Leave all three. Do not average them.
- Price conflicts on the same day. Kingsyard store: **$37.99** (product page; cart UI present). Amazon green ASIN B09PH4FNZS: **$28.99**, in stock that day. One store price is one store that day, not a survey.
- Brand product page body was empty on fetch. Specs that matter (opening size, baffle diameter, tray) are not confirmed on a filled manufacturer table tonight. Amazon bullets fill the gap; treat them as retailer copy of brand claims.
- The Spruce said smaller squirrels might still reach the cage, and that birds may use it less than an open suet feeder because access is harder. Cage openings that stop squirrels also stop larger birds.
- Cardinal perch is not a real ring on this product. Cardinals are not on Amazon’s target list. Treat cardinal access as false for this cage.
- Cornell FeederWatch: squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders; clean seed or suet feeders about every week or two; if you live with bears, do not feed except when bears hibernate. Capsaicin seed treatments are unproven for bird safety on that page.
- We are not treating Kingsyard’s own marketing blog “30-day test” as an independent lab. Brand marketing is not Bob Vila / The Spruce / Birds & Blooms.

## Who should skip

- You need woodpeckers the size of a large pileated that will not fit the openings, or cardinals on a perch. This cage is sized for small clinging birds.
- You want a weight-activated tube or a motorized spinner as the main product. Those are different mechanisms.
- You want a pole-and-dome system as the main product. This is a hang suet cage.
- Your problem is tiny red squirrels or chipmunks that may fit openings an adult grey squirrel cannot. The Spruce flagged smaller squirrels as a possible reach risk.
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears hibernate.
- You need a manufacturer page that prints a full inch table without falling back to a retailer listing.

## Verdict

If you want a second caged hang suet feeder after Squirrel-X, this is the Kingsyard metal cage with a weather dome and two-cake capacity. The Spruce’s tested roundup liked it for keeping squirrels off suet. Opening size and same-day price disagree across pages. Cornell still treats “squirrel-proof” as a claim squirrels often beat. Buy it as a cage plus placement, not as a spell.
