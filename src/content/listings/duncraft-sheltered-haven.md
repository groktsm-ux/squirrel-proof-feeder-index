## Drawbacks

- Stock conflict left unresolved: schema **InStock** / Shopify `available: true` vs visible **backordered** text.
- Material conflict left unresolved: specs **Recycled Plastic** vs body **all-metal** cage and **perforated aluminum** tray. The page doesn’t say which part is recycled plastic.
- Feed conflict left unresolved: specs **Mealworm & Insect Foods** vs body “seed or mealworms.”
- The “1-1/2"” cage figure isn’t explained on the page; mesh opening size is **unknown**.
- One cup is small. Expect frequent refills.
- It’s a tray. Cornell notes tray feeders collect droppings that other birds then eat, and suggests retiring tray feeders during disease outbreaks (for example conjunctivitis or trichomoniasis). Clean seed feeders every week or two, more often in heavy use or wet weather.
- Window placement: Cornell’s squirrel advice is about **10 feet** from jump-off points like trees or buildings; a window feeder is on a building by definition. The page prints no clearance number. Here the cage has to do the work. We don’t know how well it does that.
- Window strikes: Cornell recommends marking the outside of windows with repeating patterns spaced no more than 2 inches apart, outside screens, and dimming lights at night. The Duncraft page doesn’t address collisions.
- Suction-cup hold on your glass, in your weather, is untested by us.
- Manufacturer “deters” language is a claim. Cornell: squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders.
- No independent timed test of SKU **4449** turned up tonight. Bob Vila’s eight-feeder list doesn’t include it.

## Verdict

If you want small birds at the glass and you’re fine with a one-cup tray you refill and clean often, Duncraft’s **Sheltered Haven** is the first window-mount caged path on this index: suction cups, a drainage tray, and an all-metal outer cage the maker says deters gray squirrels and larger birds.
