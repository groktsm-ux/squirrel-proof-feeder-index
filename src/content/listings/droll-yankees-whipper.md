# Droll Yankees Yankee Whipper

## What it is

A hanging four-port **tube feeder with weight-sensitive collapsing perches**. Sold on the Perky-Pet store (current home of the Droll Yankees brand; drollyankees.com redirects there) as **Droll Yankees® Yankee Whipper® Squirrel-Proof Bird Feeder - 5 lb**, model / schema SKU **YCPW-180-M**. Perky-Pet prints that the perches are "calibrated to collapse under approximately **2 oz** of weight," with a curved perch design meant to let cardinals and small songbirds feed while keeping squirrels off. There is no motor, cage, or sliding shroud — each perch drops on its own spring.

Perky-Pet specs: capacity **5 lb**; dimensions **28.25"h x 8.25"w x 5.6"d**; mounting **Hang**; construction **UV-stabilized polycarbonate, metal**; made in **USA & Taiwan**; metal cap and base powder-coated **blue**; looped stainless steel hanging wire; internal baffle feeding four ports. No UPC / GTIN is printed on the page or in its schema — barcode **unknown**.

This is a different weight-activated form from what is already listed: the Brome shroud feeders, Roamwild PestOff, MAGNET, and Nature's Way Squirrel Shield close ports with a sliding sleeve or shroud; WoodLink Absolute / Absolute II and the Duncraft Tilt-Top are hoppers. The Whipper is on our motorized reject list only because it has no motor; it belongs here, under weight-activated.

This page is not a lab test. We did not hang the feeder. Specs below are from the Perky-Pet product page (and its schema.org data), the Perky-Pet instructions PDF, a Droll Yankees family manual, and Cornell FeederWatch. Bob Vila's squirrel-proof roundup tested the **Yankee Flipper**, not the Whipper; we found no independent test of the Whipper tonight. Store star ratings and customer reviews are not independent tests — we do not score them.

## Who it's for

People who want a plain tube feeder for perching songbirds where the anti-squirrel part is the perches themselves, and who can hang it far enough from rails, poles, and branches that a squirrel has to put its full weight on a perch. Perky-Pet's bird list on the page: American Goldfinch, Blue Jay, Cardinal, Carolina Wren, Chickadee, House Finch, Nuthatch, Purple Finch, Tufted Titmouse. Cardinal perch is marked **true** from the manufacturer copy (curved perches "encourage cardinals"; the family manual says each perch is "calibrated for birds up to the weight of a cardinal") — that is a claim, not something we watched.

## Standouts

- Mechanism matches the type: weight-sensitive perches that collapse, printed at about **2 oz**.
- No battery or charging, unlike the Yankee Flipper (our only motorized listing).
- **5 lb** capacity, printed consistently in the bullets and specs table.
- Heavy metal cap that "fits snugly" so squirrels cannot lift it (manufacturer copy).
- The instructions PDF prints a perch-spring replacement procedure and a full disassembly sequence for cleaning.
- Warranty per a Perky-Pet admin reply on the product page (Jul 9, 2026): **2-year** warranty on manufacturing defects plus a **lifetime** warranty against squirrel damage. That is a reply on the page, not a separate warranty document we fetched.
- **$99.99** on the Perky-Pet store on **2026-10-06** (one store, one fetch day; not a price survey).

## Drawbacks

- Buyability conflict left unresolved: the visible buy box says "**Please Sign In or Register to buy this product!**" while schema.org Offer says **InStock**.
- Red-squirrel conflict left unresolved. The Perky-Pet instructions PDF says red squirrels and chipmunks "may not be heavy enough to collapse the perch," and the Droll Yankees family manual's placement section says red squirrels and chipmunks may not be heavy enough to activate YCPW-180 — but the same manual's Whipper section says "This feeder is fantastic if you have problems with red squirrels." Both printed; we do not pick one.
- Perky-Pet's own description admits larger birds "will sometimes hang onto the collapsed perch and continue to feed" and the design "might not be the ultimate preventative."
- Replacement parts: Perky-Pet admin answers on the page say they do not sell replacement parts for this feeder, while the instructions PDF prints a spring replacement procedure. Where springs come from is **unknown**.
- Seed type is not named in the specs; a Perky-Pet Q&A answer says it "can be used with a wide variety of seed types." We left `seedTypes` empty rather than guess.
- No seed tray can be attached (Perky-Pet Q&A answer).
- No GTIN / UPC on the manufacturer page.
- Cleaning advice differs by page: product page says clean weekly (monthly at minimum), soak in **9:1** water-to-bleach for 15 minutes, no dishwasher; the instructions PDF says detergent and a **50/50** white vinegar rinse, and not to soak metal parts in it; Cornell says clean seed feeders every week or two and disinfect at one part bleach to nine parts water for 10 minutes. Printed as-is.
- Manufacturer "squirrel-proof" copy is a claim. Cornell: squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders.
- No independent timed test found tonight.

## Who should skip

- Your main squirrels are red squirrels or chipmunks — the manufacturer documents disagree with themselves on whether this works for them.
- Grackles, jays, or other heavy birds crowd your feeders and you want them fully shut out — Perky-Pet says larger birds sometimes cling to collapsed perches.
- You want a motorized spinning perch — look at the **Yankee Flipper**, already listed.
- You want a shroud or sleeve design — Brome, Roamwild, MAGNET, and Nature's Way are already listed.
- You cannot hang it clear of jump-offs. Perky-Pet page: **4 feet** off the ground, **8 feet** from anything a squirrel can jump from, hook at least **16"** from a pole. Instructions PDF: minimum **14"** from any rail, pole, or tree. Cornell: about **10 feet** from trees or buildings. We print all three and do not pick a winner.
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears hibernate.

## Verdict

Tenth weight-activated listing if you want a tube feeder whose individually sprung perches collapse under a squirrel — no shroud, no motor, not a Brome or WoodLink. Resistant, not magic: the maker itself says big birds may cling on, its own manuals disagree about red squirrels, and we found no independent test.
