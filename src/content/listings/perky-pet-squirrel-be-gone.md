# Perky-Pet Squirrel-Be-Gone Wild Bird Feeder

## What it is

A hanging six-port **tube feeder inside a spring-loaded metal cage**. Sold on the Perky-Pet store (Woodstream) as **Perky-Pet® Squirrel-Be-Gone® Wild Bird Feeder**, model **336-1SR**. The instructions PDF calls it **Model #336**; we leave both numbers as printed. Perky-Pet's description of how it works: "When squirrels try to gain access to the perches, the cage is pulled down, causing the decorative leaves to cover the ports." The springs can be set to different holes to change how much weight closes the ports (PDF). There is no motor and no sliding plastic shroud.

Perky-Pet specs table 1: Height **19.08 in**, Length **7.10 in**, Width **6.97 in**, Ports **6** (U-shaped perches), Squirrel Resistant **Yes**, Capacity **2 lb**, Materials **Metal, plastic**. Specs table 2 on the same page: Weight **1.81 lb**, Dimensions **19.08 in H x 7.9 in L x 6.97 in W**. Length **7.10** vs **7.9** on the same manufacturer page is left as printed. Clear plastic reservoir; built-in hanging cord; powder-coated leaf-pattern cage. Duncraft lists the same feeder (vendor Woodstream, SKU WS5057) with barcode **078978336001**, a **3 inch** tube and **18-1/2 inches** tall. The Perky-Pet page prints no UPC.

Why it is a different weight-activated form from what is already listed: Brome feeders, Roamwild PestOff and MAGNET use a sliding sleeve or shroud. WoodLink Absolute / Absolute II and the Duncraft Tilt-Top are hoppers. The Droll Yankees Whipper uses collapsing perches. The closest listed relative is the Nature's Way Squirrel Shield Advantage Cage (a collapsing seed shield). The Squirrel-Be-Gone is a different brand and covers its ports with metal leaves on the cage, with adjustable spring holes. It is not the Perky-Pet Squirrel Stumper (114G), which we list as caged.

This page is not a lab test. We did not hang the feeder. Specs come from the Perky-Pet product page (and its schema.org data and admin replies), the Perky-Pet instructions PDF, a Duncraft listing, one Birds & Blooms hands-on test, and Cornell FeederWatch. Store star ratings and customer reviews are not independent tests, and we do not score them.

## Who it's for

People who want a cheap hanging tube for small perching songbirds, where the cage itself is the squirrel trigger, and who are willing to adjust springs. Perky-Pet's "Best Used For" list: **Chickadees, Finches, Nuthatches, Titmice**. Cardinal perch is marked **false** because a Perky-Pet answer on the product page says: "This feeder is designed for Chickadees, finches, Nuthatches and titmice, not cardinals."

## Standouts

- The mechanism matches the bucket. The cage drops under a squirrel's weight and leaves cover the ports (Perky-Pet copy). Duncraft's copy says the same: "outer caging to drop and the chew-proof metal leaves to cover up the six feeding ports."
- Adjustable activation. The PDF says to hook the spring wire into a lower hole to raise the activation weight, keep all springs on the same setting, and adjust "in single increments each time-until you see no squirrels feeding." Duncraft: "Adjust the springs to deter larger birds."
- No battery or charging, unlike the Yankee Flipper (our only motorized listing).
- Independent data point: Birds & Blooms tester Katie Bandurski hung it among **8 feeders** in a Wisconsin yard, on a shepherd's pole with black sunflower seed. The first half of testing had no baffle and the second half had one, over about **a month**. She wrote that squirrels and chipmunks "seemed baffled about how to access the seed" because "their weight closes the ports." That is one yard over about one month, not a durability study. The article prints no model number. Its description (2 lb, metal leaves, $25) matches the 336, but the model is not confirmed by the article.
- Warranty: a Perky-Pet admin reply on the page (Jul 28, 2026) says the feeder "is covered by a 1-year warranty." That is a reply on the page, not a separate warranty document we fetched.
- Price: **$24.99** on the Perky-Pet store on **2026-10-22**. Duncraft listed it at **$69.95** the same day. That is two stores, one fetch each, not a price survey.

## Drawbacks

- Material conflict, left unresolved. The bullet says "**All-metal**, weight-activated cage." The specs table says Materials "**Metal, plastic**." A Perky-Pet admin reply (Oct 9, 2026) says: "While the cage itself is metal, the feeding ports are plastic." Birds & Blooms saw chipmunks "starting to chew at the plastic" after a few weeks.
- Springs. Birds & Blooms warns they are hard to reattach if they come loose during cleaning. Perky-Pet admin (Aug 21, 2026): if a spring or plastic clip is damaged, "the feeder should be discontinued until the component can be replaced." Replacement parts are available "for some components, depending on the specific feeder model" (admin, Aug 26, 2026). Whether parts exist for this exact model is **unknown**.
- Port size. A Perky-Pet admin reply (Oct 9, 2026) says "this model's open reservoir doesn't include a built-in way to restrict the feeding port size." It was answering a customer who reported a finch found dead inside the reservoir. The customer report is one review, not evidence. The admin statement is the maker's own.
- Buyability conflict, left unresolved. The visible buy box says "**Please Sign In or Register to buy this product!**" while the schema.org Offer says **InStock**.
- Height conflict: **19.08 in** (Perky-Pet) vs **18-1/2 inches** (Duncraft). Weight conflict: **1.81 lb** (Perky-Pet) vs **0.0 lb** (Duncraft Shopify field). Both printed.
- Naming conflict: Duncraft's title says "Squirrel Proof." Perky-Pet's copy says "squirrel-resistant." Cornell: squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders. We say resistant.
- Seed type is not named on the Perky-Pet page or by Duncraft. We left `seedTypes` empty rather than guess.
- The feeder base is not removable (Perky-Pet Q&A). Cleaning per the page and PDF is mild soap and water, at least every 2 weeks.
- The Spruce has a "Perky-Pet Squirrel-Be-Gone" pick, but the specs it prints (4 lb, 4 ports, flexports, 17.5 in) do not match this 2 lb, six-port 336. We did not use it as a test of this SKU. Bob Vila tested the Squirrel-Be-Gone II Home hopper, not this feeder.

## Who should skip

- You want to feed cardinals. Perky-Pet's own answer says it is not designed for them.
- Chipmunks or persistent chewers are your main problem. The ports are plastic, and the one independent tester saw chewing within weeks.
- You don't want to fiddle with springs, or you'll be away for long stretches without checking it (see the port-size admin note above).
- You want a motorized spinning perch. Look at the **Yankee Flipper**, already listed.
- You want an all-metal shroud tube. Brome, Roamwild, MAGNET and Nature's Way are already listed.
- You can't hang it clear of jump-offs. Perky-Pet admin replies on the same page give different clearances: "at least **10 to 12 feet**" from trees, fences or structures (Oct 9 and Jul 28, 2026), "at least **10 feet** away" (Aug 21, 2026), and "at least **18 inches** of clearance on all sides and away from anything a squirrel could jump from" (Sep 21, 2026). Cornell: squirrels can jump to feeders less than about **10 feet** from a tree or building. We print all of them and do not pick a winner.
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears hibernate.

## Verdict

Eleventh weight-activated listing if you want a low-cost six-port tube whose spring-loaded outer cage pulls down so metal leaves cover the ports, with no motor and no Brome or WoodLink parts. Resistant, not magic: the ports are plastic, the maker says it is not for cardinals, and the only hands-on test we found is one yard over about a month.
