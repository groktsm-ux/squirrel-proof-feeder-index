# Audubon Squirrel-Resistant Caged Tube Feeder

## What it is

A hanging caged **seed tube** sold under the Audubon brand as model **NATUBE3** / manufacturer # **23815**, UPC **715038302988**. Retailer copy names Woodlink and Panacea Products (Audubon) as the maker line. The mechanism is a powder-coated steel cage around an ABS plastic tube with **four** seed ports: small songbirds slip through the grid; adult squirrels and large birds are supposed to stay out. Max Warehouse (Woodlink wording) and Bradley Caldwell both print capacity **1.25 lb** of mixed seed and a **1.5 inch** cage grid. Amazon’s product description says **one quart** / “up to one quart,” and its capacity field shows **1.3 pounds**. Rustic Feathers prints **0.85 quarts (approx. 1.275 lbs)**. We are not converting those into one number.

This page is not a lab test. We did not hang one. Specs below are from retailer / distributor pages that name the SKU, one independent editorial comparison that lists this model as Best Caged, and Cornell FeederWatch. Bob Vila’s eight-feeder / one-week test did **not** include NATUBE3.

## Who it's for

People who want a **caged seed** feeder (not suet) so chickadees, finches, nuthatches, and similar small birds can feed while gray squirrels and bully birds stay outside the grid. Max Warehouse bird copy: favorite songbirds can slip in; doves, blue jays, and grackles cannot. Rustic Feathers bird list: chickadees, finches, goldfinches, juncos, kinglets, nuthatches, redpolls, siskins, sparrows, titmice, woodpeckers, wrens. Amazon excludes blue jays, mourning doves, and grackles by name. Those are manufacturer / retailer attract lists, not a census from our yard. Cardinals are not treated as fit for this cage on the pages we used. It hangs. Amazon’s attribute table also prints “Pole Mount” once — every other page we fetched describes a hanging loop / hang feeder. Leave that conflict. Cornell FeederWatch still uses about **10 feet** from trees or buildings as the usual jump-off buffer, and treats baffles as usually the better squirrel defense than “squirrel-proof” marketing.

## Standouts

- First **caged seed-tube** listing on this index. The three earlier caged pages (Squirrel-X Double Suet, Kingsyard suet, Birds Choice WC13 suet) are suet cages. This one is mixed-seed tube + steel grid.
- Mechanism is named and visible: Max Warehouse — **1.5 inch** caged grid, powder-coated metal, four feeding stations, ABS plastic tube, **1.25 lb**. Bradley Caldwell — protective grid work, ready to use, **1.25 LB CAP**, country USA on that distributor card. HB Davis — metal gridwork keeps adult squirrels and larger birds out; EZ-Clean base; **1.25#** mixed seed; brand line Panacea Products (Audubon).
- The Faunalist’s 2026 comparison named an Audubon NABBOX / caged tube Best Caged: powder-coated steel cage over polycarbonate/plastic tube, openings sized for small songbirds, excludes squirrels and larger nuisance birds. That is one editorial pick, not a timed backyard lab we re-ran. Capacity on that page prints **1.5 lb** — leave it against the **1.25 lb** / **one quart** retailer copy.
- Easy-fill / easy-clean claims appear on Max Warehouse, Bradley Caldwell, HB Davis, and Rustic Feathers (locking / lift-off top; removable interior tube on Rustic).

## Drawbacks

- Capacity and size copy conflict across pages. Capacity: **1.25 lb** (Max Warehouse, Bradley Caldwell, HB Davis) vs **one quart** / **1.3 pounds** (Amazon) vs **0.85 quarts ≈ 1.275 lbs** (Rustic) vs **1.5 lb** (The Faunalist). Dimensions: Amazon description **9.5" × 9.5" × 15"**; Amazon table **9" × 9" × 15.5"**; Rustic **2.5" tube / 9.5" cage / 15.25" height**; Bradley Caldwell package **9.75 × 10 × 17.75 in**; Max Warehouse package **10 × 10 × 17.5 in**, width **9.25 in**. Do not average them.
- Same-day price conflict. Amazon **$54.99** In Stock. Max Warehouse **$48.25** /each In Stock. Rustic Feathers sale **$102.99** (was $122.99). One store price is one store that day, not a survey.
- Brand / model naming is messy on purpose: Audubon, Woodlink, Panacea Products, NATUBE3, 23815, NABBOX (Faunalist), AUD-NATUBE3 (Rustic). We keep the SKU strings as printed.
- Cardinal perch is not a ring on this product. Cardinals are too large for the cage on the retailer framing we used. `cardinalPerch: false`.
- No Bob Vila / Birds & Blooms timed test of this exact SKU in the sources we opened. Amazon customer reviews and star ratings are not independent tests — ignored as evidence.
- Cornell FeederWatch: squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders (their photo caption even shows a squirrel inside a “squirrel-proof” cage); clean seed feeders about every week or two; if you live with bears, do not feed except when bears hibernate. Wire-mesh cages that admit only small birds are listed among ways to discourage large unwelcome birds — which also means desirable larger birds stay out.

## Who should skip

- You want cardinals, jays, or woodpeckers that will not fit the grid as primary guests. This cage is sized for small songbirds.
- You need a motorized spinner. Flipper is still the only true battery-motor spinning-perch listing on this index; no second motorized SKU with primary sources tonight.
- You want a weight-activated hang tube or a pole+baffle kit as the main product. Those are different mechanisms.
- Your problem is tiny red squirrels or juvenile grays that can squeeze a 1.5" grid. Retailer copy targets adult squirrels; we did not invent a red-squirrel guarantee.
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears hibernate.

## Verdict

If you want a caged **seed** tube after three caged **suet** listings, this Audubon / Woodlink NATUBE3 (23815) is the first one on the index. The Faunalist liked the design class for excluding squirrels and bully birds together. Capacity, dimensions, and same-day price disagree across pages. Cardinals stay outside. Cornell still treats “squirrel-proof” as a claim squirrels often beat. Buy it as a cage plus placement, not as a spell.
