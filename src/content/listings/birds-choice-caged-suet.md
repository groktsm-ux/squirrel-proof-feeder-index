# Birds Choice Squirrel Proof 13" Suet Feeder with Wire Cage

## What it is

A hanging double-suet feeder from Birds Choice, sold on the brand store as **Squirrel Proof 13" Suet Bird Feeder for 2 Cakes with Wire Cage in Recycled Plastic**, SKU **WC13-SNDSF**, UPC **789453950697**. The store pairs a taupe recycled-poly-lumber suet feeder (green roof, vinyl-coated grids, stainless screws, lift-off roof, hanging cable) with a **13"** wire cage. Cage openings are printed **1.38" × 1.38"** “for smaller birds to get in.” The cage base mounts to the feeder base and doubles as a perching plate; the slotted cage top lets the hanging cable through. Dimensions **12"L × 12"W × 13.5"H**. Weight **6 lb**. Capacity **two suet cakes**. Mount: hang. The store says the cage is designed to protect the feeder against squirrels, grackles, starlings, and other big birds. Made in the USA (brand home: Chilton, Wisconsin). Brand home also states a craftsmanship / 100% satisfaction guarantee on recycled and poly-lumber products.

This page is not a lab test. We did not hang one. Specs below are from the Birds Choice product page (and its product JSON), the brand home, and Cornell FeederWatch. We found no independent backyard test of this exact SKU on Bob Vila, The Spruce, Birds & Blooms, Wirecutter, or Reviewed while researching tonight. Bob Vila’s separate “Best Suet” pick in its general bird-feeder roundup is a different Birds Choice SKU (SNTP single-cake tail-prop). Do not treat that as a test of WC13-SNDSF.

## Who it's for

People who hang suet and want a caged sanctuary for small birds, not an open cake for woodpeckers and squirrels alike. The store’s own framing is smaller birds inside, squirrels and big birds out. It is a hang feeder. Birds Choice did not print a jump-off distance on the WC13-SNDSF page we fetched. Cornell FeederWatch still uses about **10 feet** from trees or buildings as the usual jump-off buffer, and treats baffles as usually the better squirrel defense than “squirrel-proof” marketing. Cornell also notes that wire-mesh cages sized for small birds are a real mechanism for keeping larger birds (and often squirrels) off seed or suet.

## Standouts

- Mechanism is named and visible: a wire cage around a two-cake suet feeder. Opening size is printed once on the manufacturer page: **1.38" × 1.38"**. No opening-size conflict on this SKU from the manufacturer.
- Capacity is **two suet cakes**. Dimensions and weight are on the manufacturer table. No unit fight on cake count.
- Build story is manufacturer-stated: recycled poly lumber that “never crack[s], split[s] or fade[s],” vinyl-coated grids, stainless screws, Made in the USA (Chilton, WI on the brand home). We did not invent a founding year.
- Cage base is bolted to the feeder base and acts as a perching plate (store). That is a concrete design note, not marketing fluff alone.
- Third caged listing on this index after More Birds Squirrel-X Double Suet and Kingsyard Metal Caged Suet. Different brand, still hang suet, still cage-primary.

## Drawbacks

- No independent test of WC13-SNDSF turned up in tonight’s primary-source pass. Verdict rests on manufacturer specs plus Cornell’s general cage and squirrel notes. Say so.
- Openings at **1.38"** are aimed at smaller birds. Larger suet regulars (many woodpeckers, and certainly woodpecker-sized “big birds” the cage is meant to block) may be locked out with the squirrels. Bob Vila’s separate Squirrel-X test made the same woodpecker-access complaint on a different caged suet — cite that only as a class issue, not as a test of this Birds Choice unit.
- Red squirrels and chipmunks can beat small openings on other caged suets (Kingsyard’s own FAQ said as much on a sibling listing). Birds Choice did not print a red-squirrel caveat on this page. Leave it as unknown for this SKU.
- Price on the Birds Choice store was **$73.99** on 2026-09-08. That is one store’s price that day, not a survey. Stock badge on the product page HTML showed both “In stock, ready to ship” and “Backordered, shipping soon” strings; treat live availability as unknown without a cart confirmation.
- Brand home guarantee language is craftsmanship / satisfaction on recycled and poly-lumber products. We did not invent squirrel-damage warranty exclusions; the WC13-SNDSF page did not spell a squirrel-damage warranty the way Droll Yankees PDFs do.
- Cornell still says squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders. A cage is resistant. Placement still matters.
- Suet care: Cornell warns raw suet can go rancid in heat; clean seed or suet feeders about every week or two. The manufacturer page on WC13-SNDSF did not print a cleaning interval.

## Who should skip

- You want woodpeckers or other larger clinging birds to reach suet without squeezing through a small grid. An open or upside-down suet cage is a different product.
- You need a motorized spinner, a weight-activated seed tube, or a pole-and-baffle system as the main product. Those are different mechanisms. Other listings on this index cover them.
- You will not hang it so a squirrel cannot sit on a nearby roof or branch and work the cage.
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears hibernate.
- You want an independently tested SKU tonight. This page does not invent one.

## Verdict

If you want a Made-in-USA recycled-poly double-suet feeder bolted inside a **1.38"** wire cage, and you are feeding small birds rather than woodpecker-sized ones, this is a clean third caged listing. Manufacturer specs are consistent. Independent test of this exact SKU: none found tonight. Cornell still treats “squirrel-proof” as a claim squirrels often beat. Buy it as a cage plus placement, not as a spell.
