# Brome Squirrel Buster Plus

## What it is

A hanging tube feeder from Brome. The US store calls it their largest-capacity Squirrel Buster and their most popular. It is 28 inches from base to hanger and holds 5.1 lb of seed. The mechanism is a weight-activated shroud: port openings in the shroud line up for birds; extra weight on the feeder forces the shroud down and closes the ports. Brome calls the spring weight-adjustable. Parts a squirrel can reach are described as chew-proof. The feeder takes apart by hand for cleaning. A Cardinal Ring is part of the design.

This page is not a lab test. We did not hang one. Specs below are from Brome's product page and one independent 1-week test of eight feeders.

## Who it's for

People who hang a mixed-seed tube for small and medium songbirds and are tired of squirrels sitting on it. Bob Vila's tester used it for chickadees, nuthatches, and finches, with the optional ring for cardinals. It is a hang feeder. Brome's placement copy is window, tree, deck, fence, garden, house, rain trough, or sliding doors, with 18 inches of clearance around it.

## Standouts

- Mechanism is named and visible: squirrel weight closes the ports. Brome says the spring is adjustable.
- Capacity is large for a hang tube. Brome says 5.1 lb. Bob Vila's comparison table lists 3 quarts for the same model. We are not converting those units. Both numbers are on the linked pages.
- Cardinal Ring is a real part, not a marketing adjective. Bob Vila says it does not come preinstalled and that installing it is a bit tricky.
- Brome Seed Tube Ventilation is on the product page: vents at the top so heated air can leave. Bob Vila also noted ventilation as a reason they picked it.
- Detachable by hand, no tools, for cleaning. Cornell FeederWatch wants seed feeders cleaned about every week or two.
- Lifetime Care is Brome's support line, Monday–Friday 9 a.m.–5 p.m. ET, except holidays. That is a phone promise, not a measured lifespan.
- Brome has been making wild bird feeders since 1998.

## Drawbacks

- Brome's own disclaimer: this feeder will not deter grackles, starlings, juvenile red/Douglas/flying squirrels, chipmunks, possums, bears, deer, or raccoons. They tell you to bring it in at night.
- If a squirrel can reach the ports without putting its weight on the feeder, the shroud does not close. Brome wants at least 18 inches of clearance. Cornell FeederWatch still says squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders and that baffles plus about 10 feet from a jump-off are usually the better defense.
- Bob Vila tested eight feeders for one week. That is short. Their "best overall" is a useful data point, not a durability study.
- Cardinal Ring install is fiddly, per Bob Vila.
- Hang only as the primary mount on these sources. Do not treat it as a pole system.
- Brome's US store listed **$129.25** on 2026-08-30. That is one store's price that day, not a survey. We are not ranking value.

## Who should skip

- You cannot hang it with roughly 18 inches of clear air around it (nearby roof, railing, or branch a squirrel can stand on).
- Your problem is grackles or starlings. Brome says this model does not stop them.
- You need a caged suet feeder or a motorized spinner. Those are different mechanisms. Later listings will cover them.
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears hibernate.
- You want a pole-and-baffle setup as the main product. This is a hanging tube.

## Verdict

If you want a weight-activated hang feeder and you will give it clearance, this is the default first listing. The mechanism is real. Brome publishes the animals it will not stop. Cornell still treats "squirrel-proof" as a claim squirrels often beat. Buy it as resistant hardware plus placement, not as a spell.
