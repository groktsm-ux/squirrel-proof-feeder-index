# Kingsyard Squirrel Proof Caged Ground Bird Feeder

## What it is

A low **recycled-plastic tray on pull-out legs with a metal cage on top**. Kingsyard sells it on its own store as **Kingsyard Squirrel Proof Caged Ground Bird Feeder**, item **KYBF171CP** (color variants KYBF171CP01, KYBF171CP02 and others). Kingsyard's description: "Squirrel proof design with 1-1/2" fly-through openings for smaller birds like bluebirds and chickadees." The feeding tray "stands 6 inches above the ground with metal mesh flooring for excellent drainage." You "pull out the supporting legs and attach the metal cage," and refill through a top lid.

Kingsyard specs fields: Feeder Type **Caged**, Mount Type **Freestanding**, Material **Recycled Plastic**, Dimensions **13.5"L x 10.2"W x 6"H**, Capacity **5 lbs**, Mesh Opening Size **1-1/2"**, Bird Species **Bluebirds, Chickadees, Nuthatches**, Seed Type **Fruit & Nuts, Mealworms, Mixed Seeds, Peanuts, Suet Cakes, Sunflowers**. The description on the same page says the tray measures **13.5" x 10.2" x 2.7"**. The 6" vs 2.7" height is left as printed; it may be tray-only vs overall, but the page does not say.

Why it is a different caged form from what is already listed: our other ten caged listings hang (tubes, suet cages, a domed tube) or stick to a window (Duncraft Sheltered Haven). This one stands on the ground or a deck. It is not the Kingsyard caged suet feeder (KYBF123CP), which we list separately. Duncraft sells a similar-looking caged ground platform (Ground Platform Haven Feeder); we did not list or compare it tonight.

This page is not a lab test. We did not set the feeder out. ## Who it's for

People who want to feed small ground- and low-feeding birds (Kingsyard lists bluebirds, chickadees and nuthatches) from a tray squirrels and larger birds can't walk into, and who can set it on a flat, solid spot. Kingsyard answered a customer that a Spotted Towhee "should be able to use this feeder."

## Standouts

- The mechanism matches the bucket: an exclusion cage with **1-1/2"** openings (Kingsyard description and specs; a Kingsyard answer restates "1.5" x 1.5" mesh openings").
- Raised mesh floor. The tray is 6 inches off the ground with metal mesh flooring for drainage (Kingsyard description).
- Cleaning, per a Kingsyard answer on the page: pull the cage and tray apart, wash with warm soapy water, rinse, air dry. That answer suggests about every **two weeks**. Cornell says every week or two.
- Cornell FeederWatch names "feeders surrounded by a wire mesh large enough for only small birds to get through" as one way to deter larger birds, which is what this cage does.
- No batteries or springs.
- Warranty: the Kingsyard store page shows "Lifetime Warranty / Lifetime warranty guaranteed!" in its site-wide strip. We did not fetch a separate warranty document, so the terms are **unknown** here.
- Price: **$48.99** on the Kingsyard store on **2026-10-23**. That is one store on one fetch day, not a price survey.

## Drawbacks

- The cage is not locked on. Kingsyard's own answer: "The cage is designed to sit on top without tightening the screws." If it feels loose, "lightly tighten the side screws." A loose cage is a squirrel weak point.
- Nothing anchors it. Asked how it stops squirrels flipping it, Kingsyard answered that it "does not include ground anchors or weight-based securing features" and "can still be vulnerable if squirrels attempt to push or flip it." Kingsyard suggests a solid surface or ground stakes (stakes are not included, per that answer).
- Cardinals are out. Kingsyard: "adult cardinals are typically too large to fit through 1.5" openings." Most larger woodpeckers "will likely not fit"; a Downy "may be able to access the seed if they're agile enough" (Kingsyard answer).
- The cage is not sold separately (Kingsyard answer). If you lose or bend it, you may need the whole set.
- Height conflict, left unresolved: **6"H** (specs field) vs **2.7"** (description).
- Naming: Kingsyard's title says "Squirrel Proof." Its own answers describe ways squirrels can still get at it. Cornell: squirrels often find a way into "squirrel-proof" feeders. We say resistant.
- Ground feeding has its own risks. Cornell says ground-feeding birds are at greatest risk from cats, and advises keeping the ground under feeders clean (rake or sweep waste and spoiled food). For anyone trying to keep large nuisance birds away, Cornell's advice is to "avoid platform trays, and do not spread food on the ground." The cage is the maker's answer to that; whether it is enough in your yard is unknown.

## Who should skip

- You want cardinals, jays or larger woodpeckers at this feeder.
- Your squirrels are strong and you can't stake it or set it on a solid, level surface.
- Outdoor cats roam your yard. Cornell says ground-feeding birds are most at risk.
- You want a hanging or pole feeder. Look at the other caged, pole+baffle or weight-activated listings.
- You want a motorized spinning perch. The **Yankee Flipper** is our only motorized listing.
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears are hibernating.

## Verdict

Eleventh caged listing and the first one that stands on the ground: a 5 lb recycled-plastic tray on legs under a 1-1/2 inch fly-through cage for small birds. Resistant, not magic. Kingsyard itself says the cage just sits on top, nothing anchors the feeder, and cardinals are too big to get in. We found no hands-on test of this model.
