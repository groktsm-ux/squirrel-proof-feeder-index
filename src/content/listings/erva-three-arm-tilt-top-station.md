# Erva Squirrel-Proof Bird Feeding Station with Tilt Top Baffles & Quick Connect Branches

## What it is

A ground-mounted **three-arm bird feeder pole station** sold on the Duncraft store as **Squirrel-Proof Bird Feeding Station with Tilt Top Baffles & Quick Connect Branches**, SKU **TREE6**. The Shopify vendor and the schema.org Brand on that page are both **Erva Tool & Die Company**. No barcode / GTIN is printed (**unknown**).

Duncraft says you get **seven pieces**: the three-arm pole, **three hanging baffles** "to fit your widest feeders," **two 24 inch quick connect branch hooks**, and **a squirrel baffle** for the pole. The hanging baffles are described as **weather baffles** that "keep your seed dry." Printed specs: **22 inch** arm outreach ("the widest arm outreach of any station we carry"), up to **20 lbs. per arm**, all-steel sectional pole with black powder coat, **21 inch** twister ground socket, **90 inches** tall above the ground, assembly without tools. Feeders are sold separately.

What makes it different from the pole+baffle gear already listed is the hanging weather baffle over each arm. The four-arm Erva station (**TREE3**) and the eight-hook **Erva XL Cross-Arm** come with a pole baffle only. The squirrel defense here is still the pole baffle. Duncraft does not say the hanging baffles stop squirrels by themselves, only that the kit "comes with hanging & pole baffles, so squirrels can't reach your feeders."

The product title says "**Tilt Top**" baffles, but the description never explains what tilts. The model, size, and shape of both the hanging baffles and the pole baffle are **unknown**. Erva's own store does not list a TREE6 kit. It sells the parts separately: the **Multi-Arm** pole (90 inches ground to finial, **~22"** extended reach, **20"** twist-in ground socket, **16 gauge** steel, Made in USA), a **24"** Branch Hook (**BH24**, **$9.99**), a **22"**-diameter hanging disk baffle (SB5 family), and several pole baffles. Erva's 2026 catalog lists a 22-inch three-arm extended-reach top (**ERTRIO**) and a 24-inch branch-style hanger (**FPWF3**). Whether TREE6 is built from exactly those parts is **unknown**.

This page is not a lab test. We did not install one. Specs below are from the Duncraft product page (and its schema.org / Shopify data), Erva's store and 2026 catalog, and Cornell FeederWatch. Store star ratings are not independent tests, and we do not score them.

## Who it's for

People who already plan to stop squirrels with a pole baffle and a real jump-off gap, and who also want each hanging feeder covered from rain. It also suits people who want extra perching room: the two branch hooks add two more hanging points with a twist-on fit. Seed type and capacity depend on whatever you hang. This SKU is only the pole, arms, hooks, and baffles. Cardinal perch is **unknown**, because there is no feeder tray. Cornell FeederWatch treats a baffle under pole-mounted feeders plus about **10 feet** from trees or buildings as the usual squirrel defense, and says squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders.

## Standouts

- It's the only listed pole kit that includes **hanging weather baffles** (three) along with the pole baffle.
- **22 inch** printed arm outreach, plus two **24 inch** branch hooks, gives five hanging points in total.
- **20 lbs. per arm** printed load.
- **21 inch** twister ground socket and **90 inch** height above ground, the same printed figures as the four-arm TREE3.
- Assembly without tools is printed.
- **$359.95** on the Duncraft store on **2026-10-24** (one store, one fetch day; not a price survey).

## Drawbacks

- The stock signals on Duncraft conflict, and we left that unresolved. The page shows "**backordered** and will ship as soon as it is back in stock," but the schema.org Offer says **InStock**.
- It costs **$359.95**, twice the **$179.95** that Duncraft charged for the four-arm TREE3 on 2026-10-05. Erva sells the parts separately, but we did not price a do-it-yourself build, because the exact baffle models are unknown.
- "Tilt Top" is in the title and nowhere else. What tilts, and how, is **unknown**.
- Baffle model, diameter, and material are **unknown** for both the pole baffle and the hanging baffles.
- The Shopify variant weight is **0.0 lb**, so the real shipping weight is **unknown**. No barcode is printed.
- Its relationship to Erva's own catalog is **unknown**, since erva.com has no TREE6 SKU. The socket is **21 inch** on Duncraft and **20"** on Erva's Multi-Arm page; we left both as printed.
- Duncraft files it under "Bird & Wildlife Feeder Accessories." Seed capacity doesn't apply, so `seedTypes` is empty on purpose.
- "Squirrel-proof" is the seller's claim. Cornell says squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders, and jump distance still matters.
- We found no independent timed test while researching tonight.

## Who should skip

- You don't need rain cover. The four-arm **Erva Squirrel-Proof Four Arm Station** already listed has the same printed pole height and outreach for about half the price.
- You want eight hanging points or a heavier pole. Look at the **Erva XL Cross-Arm** or **Squirrel Stopper Deluxe**, both already listed.
- You need a seed hopper with a weight shroud, a cage, or a motorized spinning perch. Those are different buckets, and the Yankee Flipper is still the only honest motorized listing.
- You need it delivered now. Duncraft showed it backordered tonight.
- You can't keep about **10 feet** of jump-off clearance (Cornell).
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears hibernate.

## Verdict

Eleventh pole+baffle listing and the first kit that ships hanging weather baffles over each arm as well as a pole baffle — worth a look if rain on your seed is as big a problem as squirrels, but it costs twice the four-arm Erva station, was backordered tonight, and has no independent test. It resists squirrels but won't stop every one, and the baffle details are unknown.
