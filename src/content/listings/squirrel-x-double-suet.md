# More Birds Squirrel-X Double Suet

## What it is

A hanging caged suet feeder from More Birds, sold on the Perky-Pet store as Squirrel-X model 38069. The store lists a 2 suet cake capacity, 9.5 in high by 10 in wide by 10 in deep, 1.95 lb, steel and aluminum, made in China. The mechanism is a powder-coated black steel cage with a pecan-colored steel lid: small songbirds are supposed to fit through the openings; grey squirrels and larger “bully” birds are not. An aluminum hanger is included. morebirds.com redirected to the Perky-Pet More Birds brand store on this fetch.

This page is not a lab test. We did not hang one. Specs below are from the Perky-Pet product page, one independent 1-week test of eight feeders, one independent ~1-month backyard test of eight feeders, and one expert-research roundup that did not hang this feeder.

## Who it's for

People who hang suet for small songbirds and want a cage between the cakes and the squirrels. The store’s bird list is American Goldfinch, House Finch, and Purple Finch. That is a manufacturer attract list, not a census from our yard. Bob Vila’s tester said it only accommodates smaller songbirds. Birds & Blooms’ tester, in a Wisconsin backyard for about a month, eventually had regular visitors after the birds figured the cage out. It is a hang feeder. Perky-Pet’s placement copy is 4 feet from the ground and 8 feet from anything a squirrel can jump from, and at least 16 inches of hook away from a pole. Cornell FeederWatch still uses about 10 feet from trees or buildings as the usual jump-off buffer.

## Standouts

- Mechanism is named and visible: a steel cage. The store says smaller songbirds easily fit through the openings and grey squirrels do not. Cage opening size in inches is unknown on the manufacturer spec table.
- Bob Vila tested eight feeders for one week. They named this model “best for suet,” said it kept squirrels out with small cage openings and a top baffle, and said the top fits so securely that squirrels cannot work it off. That is one week, not a durability study.
- Birds & Blooms’ Katie Bandurski tested eight feeders for about a month outside Milwaukee. She named it “best suet” and wrote that of the options tested, this suet feeder held up the best to squirrel and chipmunk invaders, even without a baffle. She called the metal exterior nice and thick. That is one yard, one month.
- Capacity is 2 suet cakes on both the Perky-Pet spec table and Bob Vila’s comparison table. No unit conflict on this model.
- Fill method from Birds & Blooms: unscrew the hanger at the top and slot the cakes in. Business Insider’s roundup describes a steel lid that lifts off. We are not treating those as two different products.
- Perky-Pet wants feeders cleaned weekly, or monthly at a minimum, outdoors, with gloves, soaked 15 minutes in a 9:1 water-to-bleach solution, then scrubbed with dish soap, then dried completely. They say do not use the dishwasher. Cornell FeederWatch wants seed or suet feeders cleaned about every week or two, and notes that a dishwasher works very well though not all feeders are dishwasher safe.

## Drawbacks

- The cage that stops squirrels also stops some wanted birds. Bob Vila wrote that woodpeckers are common suet feeders and that this design does not allow them, or other larger suet-feeding birds, in. They called that a pity. The same Bob Vila review also says larger birds simply reach in to access the suet. We are not picking a winner. Both sentences are on that page.
- Birds & Blooms’ tester said larger birds struggle to reach the feed, and that smaller birds did not seem to like going all the way inside. She later saw regular visitors. She also spotted a chipmunk inside; it still struggled to gnaw the suet.
- Perky-Pet’s own product name mixes “squirrel-proof” and “squirrel-resistant.” Cornell still says squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders, and that baffles plus about 10 feet from a jump-off are usually the better defense. Birds & Blooms’ squirrel-proof roundup opens the same way: there is no such thing as completely squirrel-proof.
- Hang only. Bob Vila could not pole-mount it where they first intended. Do not treat it as a pole system.
- Cardinal perch is not a part. The cage is built for small songbirds. If you want cardinals at suet, this is the wrong shape.
- Cage opening size is unknown. The manufacturer does not print the mesh in inches. We are not borrowing a number from a different caged suet brand.
- “Double” is not one fact. The store name is Double Suet (two cakes). Birds & Blooms calls it a double cage design. Business Insider describes an interior cage for the cakes and an exterior cage for squirrels. Print all three.
- Business Insider’s 2024 research roundup lists 4 feeding ports. The Perky-Pet spec table does not list a port count. That roundup is expert interviews plus retail research, not a hang test. The store still cites a 2021 Business Insider “best suet” mention. The page we fetched is dated 2024-03-11 and still names this model best suet.
- Perky-Pet store admins, answering a public question in March 2025, said they do not provide replacement parts.
- Cornell’s suet-cage note: make sure the lid is secured so no animals get stuck inside. Cornell also warns that suet can go rancid in hot weather if it has not been specially processed.
- Perky-Pet listed **$35.59** on 2026-08-31 and showed it out of stock that day. That is one store’s price that day, not a survey. We are not ranking value.

## Who should skip

- You want woodpeckers, cardinals, or other larger suet birds as the main guests. Bob Vila and Birds & Blooms both say this mesh is a small-bird cage.
- You cannot hang it. Bob Vila: not pole-mounted.
- Your problem is chipmunks that can fit through small mesh. Birds & Blooms’ tester saw one inside.
- You need a weight-activated seed tube or a motorized spinner as the main product. Those are different mechanisms.
- You live with bears. Cornell FeederWatch recommends against feeding except when bears hibernate.
- You want a feeder whose wearing parts the current store will sell you. Perky-Pet’s own Q&A says they do not provide replacement parts.
- You want a printed cage-gap spec before you buy. The manufacturer page does not give one.

## Verdict

If you want a caged suet hang feeder for small songbirds and you will live without woodpeckers on it, this is the default caged listing. Bob Vila’s one-week test and Birds & Blooms’ one-month backyard test both found it kept squirrels off the cakes. Both also found the same tradeoff: the mesh that stops squirrels stops or slows some birds. Cornell still treats “squirrel-proof” as a claim squirrels often beat. Buy it as a cage plus placement, not as a spell.
