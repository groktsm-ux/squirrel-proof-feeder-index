# Squirrel Stopper Deluxe

## What it is

A ground-mounted multi-station bird feeder **pole system** from Squirrel Stopper (sold on JCS Wildlife), not a seed hopper by itself. The squirrel defense is a factory-attached cylinder baffle on the middle pole section: JCS says the center cylinder rocks side to side and floats up and down so squirrels cannot grab it or climb past. Duncraft, selling the same family as Squirrel Stopper System (SKU **SQC05**), calls it a **27-inch** “floating” baffle that rocks and moves up and down, and also claims raccoon resistance on that page. We did not measure the baffle. JCS’s product page does not print a baffle diameter. Both descriptions stay.

Fully installed height is about **8 feet** on JCS and Duncraft. JCS says hung feeders sit around **5–6 feet** off the ground. Crossbars give **four** hanging stations about **4 inches** from the center pole and another **four** about **16 inches** out — eight points total. Colors on the JCS page: Bronze and Black. Made in China (JCS). Brand address on the instructions PDF: Squirrel Stopper LLC, Evansville IN.

This page is not a lab test. We did not install one. Specs below are from the JCS product page and Shopify variants that day, the Deluxe instructions PDF, the JCS Squirrel Stopper warranty block, one Duncraft product page, The Spruce’s roundup (direct fetch 200 tonight; they say they have not hang-tested this pole), Bird Watching HQ and Garden Experiments backyard write-ups, and Cornell FeederWatch.

## Who it's for

People whose main problem is squirrels climbing a feeder pole, who want one station that can hang several feeders (or houses / baskets / chimes, per The Spruce’s copy), and who can plant the auger with a real jump-off buffer. Seed type and capacity are whatever you hang — this SKU is the pole and baffle. Cardinal perch is unknown; there is no feeder tray on the pole itself. Cornell FeederWatch still treats baffles plus about **10 feet** from trees or buildings as the usual squirrel defense, and still says squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof gear.

## Standouts

- Mechanism matches the type: a pole baffle is the product. Cornell’s usual advice is baffles under pole-mounted feeders; this system builds the baffle into the middle tube.
- Eight hanging points on two 1-inch OD cross tubes (JCS / Duncraft / The Spruce table). Duncraft adds arms **36 inches** across overall and a **2-inch** diameter pole.
- Tool-light assembly claims. JCS: screw-in steel auger, twist-and-lock tube sections, no tools required on the product blurb. The PDF still ships hex bolt, wrenches, and thumbnuts — print both.
- Placement language on every manufacturer surface we fetched is explicit about a **10-foot** buffer (wording differs; see Drawbacks).
- One-year Squirrel Stopper warranty against material or manufacturer defects when install rules are followed (JCS warranty page). Stainless-steel springs guaranteed one year not to rust, corrode, or deform under normal use (PDF). Duncraft’s own store guarantee is one year from purchase — different seller, different warranty.
- The Spruce named this Best Squirrel-Proof Bird Feeder Pole and wrote they love the design, while stating they have **not** tested this anti-squirrel feeder yet. That is an award pick without a Spruce hang test.
- Bird Watching HQ’s Scott writes that he personally owns and loves this pole, has never seen squirrels get past the baffle, and that install took about half an hour. Crossbars about **7 feet** up. He warns heavy hoppers can make it lean. One owner, not a lab.
- Garden Experiments reported no squirrels for three years, then a 2015 move note: too close to a tree and squirrels leaped over; moved about two feet farther and they stopped. A 2018 note still found the pole usable after about seven years with rust paint. One yard over years.

## Drawbacks

- This is not a feeder. You buy seed capacity with whatever you hang. Empty `seedTypes` on purpose.
- Weight-limit conflict. Deluxe instructions PDF: **15 lb** per hanger, **60 lb** total across four hangers. Duncraft: **30 lb** capacity per arm. JCS product blurb does not print a weight number. We are not picking a winner.
- Hardware-count conflict. JCS product includes list: **4** eyebolts and nuts. Deluxe PDF contents: **8** eyelet screws, lock washers, and thumbnuts, plus support brackets. Leave both.
- Price and stock conflict on the same fetch day. JCS Shopify variants: Bronze SKU **082045036381** at **$249.49**; Black SKU **855584001017** at **$254.49**. Product page showed Sold out. Duncraft listed **$299.95** with Add to cart. The Spruce Amazon offer block showed **$255** on direct fetch tonight (earlier crawl excerpts had ~$245). Those are one-store prices that day, not a survey. Hang-height conflict: JCS ~5–6 ft; Bird Watching HQ crossbars ~7 ft; Garden Experiments ~6.5 ft after sinking — leave all three.
- Placement wording expands across documents. JCS product: install **10 feet from poles or walls**. Warranty: **ten feet from any bushes, trees, shrubs, buildings, etc.**, baffle at least **30 inches** off the ground and free to move, feeders must not touch or hang below the baffle. PDF: do not install within **10 feet** of trees, fences, house roof, or any other overhang or elevated platform. Print all three. Do not merge them.
- Baffle diameter is **27 inches** on Duncraft and unknown on the JCS product page and PDF we fetched. Leave both.
- Manufacturer marketing still says “100% squirrel proof.” Cornell FeederWatch: squirrels often find a way into so-called squirrel-proof feeders. Baffles are usually the better defense, and jump distance still matters.
- Raccoon claim appears on Duncraft’s page, not on the JCS product copy we fetched. Leave that as a retailer claim.
- Auger install can need two people and soft or loosened soil (PDF warns about bending the temporary bar; customer review language on JCS mentions two people). Heavy metal with sharp edges per PDF warnings.
- Old squirrelstopper.com product URL still 404s; brand sales path is JCS Wildlife. PDF still points at squirrelstopper.com for installation details.

## Who should skip

- You only need one hanging tube feeder and already have a working pole baffle. Sky Cafe is the dome-style pole+baffle listing; this is a multi-arm station.
- You cannot keep about **10 feet** from a jump-off (trees, roof, fence, wall, building). Manufacturer and Cornell both care about that distance. Garden Experiments showed the leap-over failure when too close to a tree.
- You want a motorized spinner, a weight-activated tube, or a caged suet feeder as the main product. Those are different mechanisms. Earlier listings cover them.
- You need a single printed price, a single weight limit, or a single eyebolt count before you buy. Stores and the PDF disagree.
- Your yard has bears. Cornell recommends against any feeding where bears are active except in hibernation season.
- You expected a seed hopper in the box. It is not included.

## Verdict

If you want a second pole+baffle path and you can plant an 8-foot multi-station pole with a floating cylinder baffle and a real 10-foot buffer, this is the default multi-arm station listing. Bird Watching HQ and Garden Experiments both report strong baffle performance in their yards when placement is right. The Spruce likes the design and has not hang-tested it. Cornell still wants baffles plus distance, and still treats “squirrel-proof” as a claim. Buy the baffle, the clearance, and the hung feeders together. Resistant, not magic.
